no scary math, no heavy equations — just the fun, intuitive secret of how AI actually talks.

the 30-second idea

An LLM (Large Language Model) is the world's most powerful autocomplete.

You know how when you type "Happy..." on your phone keyboard, it suggests "Birthday" or "New Year"? An LLM does the exact same thing — but it has read almost the entire internet! It doesn't "think" or have feelings. It plays one simple game over and over:

"Looking at all the words written so far, what is the single most likely next word piece?"

🧷 the golden rule
AI generates text one tiny piece at a time. When ChatGPT gives you a 100-word explanation, it didn't write it in one flash. It guessed word #1, added it, guessed word #2, added it, and repeated that loop 100 times!

how an LLM talks: the guessing loop

Here is what happens behind the scenes every single time you hit enter:

tokens: the lego bricks of AI

Computers cannot read human letters directly. But human languages have millions of distinct words (and people make up new slang like "rizz" and "delulu" every week!).

Instead of memorizing every word, AI breaks text into bite-sized Lego bricks called Tokens:

  • Common words are usually 1 token: "cat", "pizza", "happy".
  • Long or unusual words get split into pieces: "unbelievable" $\to$ [un] [believ] [able].
  • Rule of thumb: In English, 1 token is roughly 3/4 of a word (or about 4 characters). A 75-word paragraph is about 100 tokens.

⚠️ fun fact
Ever wonder why ChatGPT used to struggle with "How many r's in strawberry?"
Because the AI never sees the individual letters! It only sees two Lego bricks: [straw] and [berry]. Asking it to count characters inside a token is like asking someone to count how many screws are inside a sealed toaster!

temperature & sampling: the creativity knob

When the model predicts the next word, it doesn't just have one answer. It creates a list of possibilities with percentages.

Temperature ($T$) is the creativity slider:

  • ❄️ Cold ($T = 0.0$ to $0.2$): The boring robot mode. It always picks the #1 most obvious word with 100% certainty. Zero surprises. Perfect for code, math, and factual answers!
  • ✨ Warm ($T = 0.7$): The sweet spot. Used by ChatGPT for chatting. Mostly sticks to good words, but adds enough spice to sound natural and human.
  • 🔥 Super Hot ($T \ge 1.5$): The crazy chaos mode. Rare, bizarre words get boosted. The model starts rambling and making up wild nonsense (hallucinations!).

context window: the goldfish memory desk

Does ChatGPT remember what you told it last week? No!

An LLM does not have a human brain that stores memories while it sleeps. Instead, it has a small desk called the Context Window:

  • Imagine a desk that can only hold 8 sheets of paper.
  • Every time you say something, you put a sheet on the desk. Every time the AI answers, it puts another sheet on the desk.
  • What happens when the desk is full? If you add sheet #9, sheet #1 falls off into the trash can!
  • This is called FIFO eviction (First In, First Out). Once a message falls off the desk, the AI has 100% forgotten it ever existed!

attention: the brain's flashlight

Words are chameleons. The exact same word can mean completely different things depending on context:

  • "I sat on the river bank." (dirt edge of water)
  • "I put money in the bank." (financial building)

How does a computer know which meaning to use? Through Attention!

Think of Attention as a flashlight. When the model reads a word like "it", its flashlight shines backward across the whole sentence to illuminate which word "it" is referring to.

"attention is all you need" — the paper that changed the world

In 2017, a team of Google researchers published a legendary paper titled "Attention Is All You Need". It introduced the Transformer architecture, which powers ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and every modern AI model today.

why was it so revolutionary?

Before 2017, older AI models read text like a first-grader pointing a finger at a book: one word, then the next, then the next. It was painfully slow and couldn't run in parallel across modern computer chips.

The Transformer tossed that out the window! It said: "Why read sequentially? Let's look at all words at the same time and connect them with Attention!"

📚 the youtube library analogy
Inside the Transformer, every word looks up information using three simple things:
  • Query (Search): What you type into the YouTube search bar (e.g. "how to bake cookies").
  • Key (Title/Tag): The video title on YouTube (e.g. "Grandma's Cookie Recipe").
  • Value (Content): The actual video you watch!
Attention simply checks how well your Query matches each Key, and grabs the Values of the best matches!

quick check: test what you learned!

1. Does ChatGPT write an entire essay in one single go?
Nope! It writes one token at a time. It predicts the next word piece, slaps it on the end, and loops until it decides to stop.
2. What temperature would you set if you want an AI to write Python code?
Near zero (e.g. 0.0 or 0.1)! In coding, you want the single most accurate, correct syntax. You don't want the AI being "creative" and making up fake programming commands!
3. If your conversation gets too long, what happens to the oldest messages?
They get evicted from the context window! Just like papers falling off a small desk, once messages slide out of the buffer, the AI has completely forgotten them.
4. Why was the 2017 Transformer paper called "Attention Is All You Need"?
Because it proved you don't need slow step-by-step reading (RNNs)! Simply letting words look at each other with Attention was faster, smarter, and trained much better on modern computers.
