the secret geometry of language: how AI turns words into coordinates in 3d space and navigates meaning.

the 30-second idea

An Embedding is a GPS coordinate for meaning.

In Topic 01, we saw that AI turns words into Tokens like #4219. But raw numbers are dumb. Token #4219 has no clue that it's related to #4220, just like house number 12 doesn't know what kind of food the neighbor eats.

To do math on human thought, computers convert every word into a list of numbers called a Vector (for example, $[0.82, -0.41, 0.65]$). You can think of these numbers as coordinates in an invisible multi-dimensional room:

Words with similar meanings point in similar directions. The closer two vectors point, the more related their concepts are!

🧷 the golden rule of embeddings
Meaning is geometry. In an embedding space:
  • Proximity = Similarity: "cat" and "kitten" sit right next to each other.
  • Direction = Concept: Moving in the "royalty" direction takes you from "man" to "king" and from "woman" to "queen".
  • Angle = Relatedness: We measure similarity using the angle between arrows (Cosine Similarity), not how long the arrow is.

why token ids fail: the need for coordinates

Imagine if we gave every word a one-hot ID number or an isolated slot. The word "puppy" would be just as distant from "dog" as it is from "submarine" or "refrigerator"! The computer would have zero intuition that puppies and dogs are both furry four-legged pets.

lab 2: cosine similarity — why angles measure meaning

Once words are vectors, how do we tell if two words mean the same thing? You might guess: "Just measure the straight-line ruler distance (Euclidean distance) between the points!"

🚨 the trap: why euclidean distance fails for text
Suppose you have:
  • A 500-page book about chocolate cake recipes.
  • A 1-sentence tweet: "Baking a yummy chocolate cake with cocoa!"
Because the book mentions chocolate words 5,000 times, its vector arrow is enormously long. The tweet's vector arrow is very short. The straight-line distance between their arrow tips is massive! But they are talking about the exact same topic!
Solution: We ignore arrow length (magnitude) and look only at the Angle $\theta$ between the arrows. That is Cosine Similarity!

The mathematical formula is simple: normalize both arrows to length $1$ and compute their dot product:

$$\text{Cosine Similarity} = \cos(\theta) = \frac{\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}}{\|\mathbf{u}\| \|\mathbf{v}\|}$$

quick check: test your intuition

1. Why can't we just measure distance between words with a regular ruler (Euclidean distance)?
Because longer documents or higher word frequencies stretch the vector arrow outward without changing its conceptual topic. By using Cosine Similarity, we only compare the direction the arrow points, rendering the comparison immune to length differences.
2. How does a modern LLM handle a word like "apple" that has two completely different meanings?
Through contextual embeddings! In modern Transformer architectures, the surrounding words (e.g. "pie, cinnamon" vs "iPhone, NASDAQ") pull and rotate the word's vector toward the appropriate semantic cluster before generation begins.
3. How does this lead directly into our next topic: Attention?
Embeddings give every word an initial vector in space. Attention is the dynamic flashlight mechanism where vectors compute dot products against each other to decide how much they should update their positions based on surrounding words!
