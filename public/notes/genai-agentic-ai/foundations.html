the foundations: autocomplete at planetary scale, tokens, creativity dials, and memory desks.

the 30-second idea

An LLM (Large Language Model) is the world's most powerful autocomplete.

When you type "Happy..." on your phone keyboard, it suggests "Birthday" or "New Year". An LLM operates on that identical premise — but it has digested hundreds of billions of words from across the web. It possesses no feelings, no consciousness, and no secret mind. It runs one loop continuously:

"Given all previous tokens, what is the single most probable next token?"

🧷 the golden rule
AI generates text one tiny piece at a time. When ChatGPT outputs a 200-word essay, it never constructs it in one flash. It calculates token #1, attaches it to the prompt, re-reads everything, calculates token #2, and loops until it hits a stop token.

how an LLM talks: the generation loop

Here is what happens underneath the hood every time you send a message to an AI model:

tokens: the lego bricks of language

Computers do not consume English words directly. But human dictionaries contain hundreds of thousands of words, and humans continuously invent new slang.

Modern models use Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) to slice text into reusable fragments called Tokens:

  • Common words form a single token: "apple", "robot", "learn".
  • Long or infrequent words are segmented: "unbelievable" $\to$ [un] [believ] [able].
  • Rule of thumb: In English, 1 token $\approx$ 0.75 words (about 4 characters). 1,000 tokens is roughly 750 words.

⚠️ strawberry gotcha
Why did early models fail to answer "How many r's in strawberry?"
Because the model never inspects raw letters! The tokenizer chunked the word into two tokens: [straw] and [berry]. Counting internal characters requires the model to decode token IDs into spellings via post-training.

temperature: the creativity & randomness knob

When an LLM prepares to output the next word, it assigns percentages to all the candidates. Temperature ($T$) is a dial that controls how much risk or randomness the model is allowed to take:

  • ❄️ Cold ($T = 0.0$ to $0.2$): The Boring Robot. Always picks the #1 most obvious word with near 100% certainty. Zero surprises. Perfect for Python code, math, and factual queries!
  • ✨ Warm ($T = 0.7$): The Chat Sweet Spot. Mostly sticks to good words, but adds enough variety to sound natural and conversational (used by ChatGPT).
  • 🔥 Super Hot ($T \ge 1.4$): Chaos & Hallucinations. Weird, unexpected words get boosted. The model starts rambling and making things up!

context window: the finite working desk

Does a language model remember conversations after a session concludes? No.

Each interaction is stateless. The model's active working memory is limited to its Context Window:

  • Picture a desk that holds only 4 message cards at once.
  • As conversation progresses, older message cards get pushed off the edge (FIFO eviction).
  • Once a message is evicted from the context window, the model cannot access or recall it unless retrieved via external mechanisms like RAG or memory stores.

quick check: test your understanding

1. Why does an LLM produce text incrementally rather than all at once?
LLMs are autoregressive: every newly generated token becomes part of the input prompt for predicting the subsequent token.
2. What happens if temperature is set to $T = 0$?
The model deterministically selects the argmax token (highest raw logit score) every step with zero randomness.
3. How does the model know which earlier words are relevant when generating?
Through Attention! Instead of reading words sequentially, Attention computes relevance weights between every pair of tokens. That is our exact next topic.
