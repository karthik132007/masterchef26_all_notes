seven floors, one job each — learn the building, own networking.

the 30-second idea

The OSI model splits networking into 7 layers, each with exactly one job. A message travels down the sender's stack (each layer wraps it a little more), crosses the wire as bits, then travels up the receiver's stack (each layer unwraps its own part). Stare at the map below until the direction feels obvious — that is 80% of the chapter.

🧷 remember
Please Do Not Throw Sausage Pizza Away — 7 Application down to 1 Physical. Say it once and the order sticks forever.

the 7 layers in 7 lines

  • 7 · Application — what apps touch. HTTP, DNS, FTP.
  • 6 · Presentation — format, compress, encrypt. JPEG, TLS.
  • 5 · Session — opens, runs and closes conversations. Sockets.
  • 4 · Transport — safe end-to-end delivery + ports. TCP, UDP. Unit: segment.
  • 3 · Network — logical addressing + picking the route. IP, routers. Unit: packet.
  • 2 · Data Link — frames for a single hop, MAC addresses. Ethernet, switches. Unit: frame.
  • 1 · Physical — raw bits onto the wire. Cables, hubs. Unit: bits.

OSI vs the TCP/IP model

Exams love this mapping. The real world mostly uses 4 layers — the top three OSI layers collapse into one:

TCP/IP (4)Covers OSI
Application7 + 6 + 5 (all the talking-to-apps work)
Transport4 (TCP / UDP)
Internet3 (IP)
Link2 + 1 (Ethernet, cables)
⭐ exam tip
Three facts cover most questions: order 7→1 (mnemonic above), switch = L2 (MAC), router = L3 (IP), and units: segment → packet → frame → bits going down.
⚠️ gotcha
TCP is L4, IP is L3 — people fuse them because the suite is called "TCP/IP". And HTTPS encryption (TLS) sits around L6, above TCP — that is why it can secure anything TCP carries.

quick check

  • A switch or a router — which layer each? Switch = L2, forwards by MAC. Router = L3, forwards by IP.
  • Where does your "Hello" gain its IP header? L3, going down the sender's stack. The receiver's L3 strips it back off.
  • Which layer cares about ports like :443? L4 Transport — ports are how TCP/UDP tell apps apart.

the 20-second answer

OSI splits networking into 7 layers with one job each: apps, format, sessions, delivery, routing, frames, bits. Senders wrap headers going down; receivers strip them coming up. Switches live at L2, routers at L3, TCP at L4 — and the real-world TCP/IP model just folds the top three layers into one.
